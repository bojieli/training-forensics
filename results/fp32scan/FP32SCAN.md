# Public fine-tunes stored in fp32: what their weights show

Generated by `scripts/fp32scan/report.py`. Wording follows STYLE.md. Each token in a model's vocabulary has its own
vector of numbers (its embedding); a token that never appears in training keeps a vector bit-identical to the base
model. In an fp32 release every change keeps its full precision, so the size and timing of updates can be estimated.

## 2. s1-32B and s1.1-32B (simplescaling)

Base Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (bf16), release fp32, training data public (s1K / s1K-1.1, 1,000 documents each),
training script public (`train/sft.sh`: 5 epochs, batch 16 on 16 GPUs, cosine, 5% warm-up, peak 1e-5,
Adam betas 0.9/0.95, weight decay 1e-4). The token-set comparison with the published data is reported by the
published-data lane (results/published); here only the parts that need fp32.

### s1-32B

- **Storage:** 16,573 token vectors changed, 135,491 bit-identical to the base. 99.998% of the changed numbers are off the 16-bit grid, so the training kept fp32 master weights and the release carries full precision.
- **Weight decay:** all 135,491 never-updated vectors are bit-identical to the base. With lr x wd <= 1e-9 per step, the per-step factor 1 - lr x wd rounds to exactly 1.0 in fp32, so the published weight decay of 1e-4 cannot change any number; the weights agree. (No replay or +-1-step control is possible: there is no decay to replay.)
- **Shuffle seed and batch size:** 5,963 tokens appear in exactly one document. Under the Hugging Face Trainer convention (epoch e order = torch.randperm(1000) seeded with seed + e), every candidate (seed, batch size) predicts when each such token was updated, and so how far its vector moved. Of 2001 seeds x batch sizes [8, 16, 32], the best fit is **seed 42, batch 16** (315 steps): median misfit 0.029 vs 0.074 for the typical seed and 0.063 at the best 1% of all candidates. The runner-up is seed 43, which shares 4 of 5 epoch orders with seed 42 (seed + e). Seed 42 is the Trainer default; the script sets none.
- **Schedule (seed fixed):** all 8 best fits are cosine (no linear or constant schedule, and no beta2 of 0.98, 0.99 or 0.999, reaches the top 8); warm-up 16 steps of 315 (published: 5% = 16) fits best but 13-19 are within noise; Adam beta2 0.95. Estimated peak learning rate 9.37e-06 (published 1e-5): low by 6% because the size model assumes equal gradient sizes at each of a token's five updates.
- **Single documents:** with the seed known, every document's 5 training steps follow from the shuffle. Checked one document at a time (documents with >=3 single-document tokens, n=527), seed 42's step pattern beats all 500 other seeds for 7 documents and is in the best 5% for 108 (chance: 26). The seed is identified from all documents together; individual document steps are implied by it, not independently confirmed.

## 5. Limits

- Step-level timing needs single- or few-update tokens; RL and large SFT runs touch every token many times.
- A release stored as fp32 is not necessarily trained in fp32: check whether its values sit on the 16-bit grid.
- The s1 size model ignores per-update gradient size; it identifies the seed and schedule shape, and estimates the peak learning rate within ~10%.
