# Pretraining learning-rate schedules recovered from public checkpoint series

**Status: partial, stopped early** (the project was refocused), then the claims below were re-checked with exact
16-bit bounds (`bounds.py`, `verify.py`; see *Validation*). The scripts are in `scripts/series/`.
Every number below is generated by `scripts/series/report.py` from the JSON files in this folder.

## Method

Every token in a model's vocabulary has its own vector of numbers (its embedding). If a token never appears in
training, its vector is never updated by gradients. With decoupled weight decay (AdamW), the vector is still
shrunk at every step by a factor (1 - lr x wd). Between two saved checkpoints, the vector is therefore multiplied
by P = prod(1 - lr_t x wd). We call -ln P (about wd x the sum of the learning rates over those steps) the
**total weight decay applied** in that interval. Given the published weight decay, dividing by wd and by the
number of steps gives the mean learning rate in that interval, with no access to logs.

Steps: (1) `detect.py` compares an early and a late checkpoint and keeps the token vectors whose late value
is an exact rescaling of the early one (never updated). (2) `series.py` fetches only those vectors from every
checkpoint (HTTP range reads; no full downloads) and measures the shrink factor per interval.
(3) `analyze.py` fits warm-up + cosine / linear / constant schedules and compares with the published recipe.

## Findings

1. **OLMoE-1B-7B**: the schedule is recovered to 0.08% rms per interval: cosine, peak 4.0e-4, floor 0.1 x peak, decay planned for 5.0T tokens (training ran to 5.13T).
2. **OLMo-7B-0724**: the published schedule (HF card and the run's config `OLMo-7B-0724.yaml`) is reproduced to a median of 1.0000 over 818 intervals, including OLMo's warm-up from 0.1 x peak. An earlier version of this report said the GitHub config was contradicted: it had compared against the wrong file (the February 2024 OLMo-7B config). Withdrawn.
3. **Amber**: the never-updated token vectors do not shrink at all for the first 259 data chunks, exactly while the checkpoints are bf16; they shrink as the published cosine predicts once the checkpoints become fp32. This is consistent with weights held in 16-bit during training in that phase (each step's decay is too small to survive 16-bit rounding); no decay on the embedding in that phase cannot be excluded.
4. **Apertus-8B**: the WSD stable learning rate is recovered as 1.1e-4 (common range of 36 intervals: 1.09975e-4-1.10009e-4, assuming the published wd 0.1), constant across the batch-size doubling.
5. **Viking**: within 1% of the card in 8 of 9 intervals; one interval (600B-700B tokens) is 3.7% low, cause unknown. **K2**: decay consistent with a cosine, but the floor is not determined (fits give 0.05-0.09; the earlier 0.092 vs 0.1 comparison is withdrawn).
6. **OLMo 2** (1B-32B) and **OLMo 3** stage 1 put no weight decay on the embedding, so their schedules cannot be recovered this way.

## Caveats

- The learning rate is only recovered relative to the weight decay (the data give lr x wd). Every LR above assumes the published wd.
- Exact bounds (`bounds.py`): each stored value stands for the interval of reals that round to it, on the grid the values actually sit on (K2's fp16 files hold bf16 values); fp32 series add the worst-case accumulated fp32 rounding of the training steps. Intervals whose vectors are not pure rescalings (Apertus cool-down) are reported as inconsistent and not used.
- The warm-up is only constrained when a checkpoint falls inside it (OLMo-7B-0724: one interval).
- Amber/K2 checkpoints are numbered by data chunk, not by step. Steps per chunk are inferred from the published peak LR.
- OLMo-1B-0724 and TinyLlama were not fitted (fetch stopped early).
